Project Overview

Project Overview
This project is a comprehensive social media audit of Auburn University, designed to evaluate how effectively the university uses major social platforms to support its marketing and communication objectives. The audit will focus on Auburn’s presence across Instagram, Facebook, X, Linkedin, and TikTok. By examining profile details, content types, and levels of engagement, the project will build a clear picture of Auburn’s current social media performance.
In addition to evaluating Auburn’s owned channels, the audit will compare the university’s activity and strategy to those of key competitor institutions. This competitive lens will help highlight Auburn’s strengths, weaknesses, and opportunities within the higher-education landscape, where social media is a critical tool for showcasing campus life, academic programs, research, and brand identity. 
The ultimate goal of this project is to translate observational data into clear, evidence-based conclusions and actionable recommendations. Using audience personas, competitive insights, and platform analysis, the report will outline where Auburn University’s social media strategy is working well and where there is room for improvement.

Introduction

Auburn and Social Media
Auburn University, founded in 1859, has grown into one of the largest universities in the South and remains committed to its land-grant mission. With more than 31,500 students, 150+ academic programs, and over 5,400 full-time employees, Auburn emphasizes an exceptional student experience, impactful research, innovation, and strong community engagement. These priorities are reflected in Auburn’s 2035 strategic plan, Grounded and Groundbreaking, which focuses on academic excellence, student success, and strengthening the Auburn brand.
Within the higher-education industry, social media plays a significant role in shaping public perception and influencing prospective students. Universities compete across major platforms by sharing content related to academics, campus life, research accomplishments, faculty expertise, and student achievements. The standard expectation for institutions in this space is to deliver a consistent mix of informative, engaging, and community-driven content that reflects the campus experience and supports institutional goals.
For Auburn University, social media serves as both a communication channel and a strategic marketing tool. Current students, prospective students, parents, alumni, and faculty rely on these platforms to learn about the university, stay informed, and interact with the Auburn community. Strong social media performance helps Auburn raise awareness, highlight its distinctiveness, and maintain meaningful connections with its audiences. This audit evaluates how effectively Auburn is using its platforms to meet these goals and how its efforts compare to those of key competitors.

Persona 1: Current Student
Mya Turner
Mya is a 19-year-old sophomore majoring in Pre-Business who is active in campus clubs, Greek life, and intramurals. She uses Auburn’s social media channels to stay updated on campus events, academic deadlines, and new opportunities. A strong sense of school spirit and a desire to feel connected drive much of her online behavior, along with a fear of missing out on important information or experiences.
Mya prefers Instagram and TikTok, where she looks for short, energetic videos, clear announcements, student success stories, and behind-the-scenes campus life. She also engages with athletics content and relatable posts about student life. Social media is a key way she builds a sense of belonging at Auburn while keeping an eye out for internships and resources that support her academic and career goals.

Persona 2: Prospective Student
Jordan Smith
Jordan is a 17-year-old high school senior planning to major in Engineering and exploring whether Auburn is the right fit for him academically, socially, and financially. He uses Auburn’s social media channels to understand what daily life on campus looks like, get a sense of the culture and community, and evaluate academic programs, safety, and student resources. Jordan is motivated by Auburn’s engineering reputation, opportunities for scholarships and internships, and the appeal of joining a spirited, supportive college environment.
He spends most of his time on Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube, where he looks for authentic day-in-the-life videos, student vlogs, dorm and dining hall tours, and clear explanations of majors and admissions steps. Jordan connects strongly with emotional storytelling and student success narratives, which help him picture himself on campus and decide whether Auburn is the place where he can thrive.

Persona 3:
Alum
Dr. Emily Carter
Dr. Emily Carter is a 42-year-old Regional Marketing Director and Auburn alumna from the Class of 2005. She stays engaged with Auburn to maintain a connection to its culture, traditions, and ongoing successes. Emily uses Auburn’s social channels to follow major university updates, stay involved in the alumni network, and find opportunities to support the institution through donations, mentorship, or professional connections. Her motivations come from a strong emotional attachment to Auburn and pride in the university’s continued growth and achievements.
Emily is most active on Facebook, LinkedIn, and Instagram, where she looks for alumni success stories, updates on major initiatives, and content that highlights Auburn’s traditions and history. She also responds well to posts that offer meaningful ways to give back or stay involved. For Emily, social media is a way to stay connected to the Auburn community long after graduation while continuing to invest in its future.

Competitive Analysis

Competitor 1:
Alabama
Type: Rival Competitor
Overview: The University of Alabama (UA) is the state’s flagship public institution and one of Auburn’s primary rivals in academics, athletics, and brand identity. With a large student body, strong national visibility, and a well-established athletic culture, UA’s social media presence reflects both statewide pride and broad national appeal- especially around sports.

Competitor 1:
Alabama
Facebook: The University of Alabama’s Facebook presence is one of its strongest platforms, marked by high engagement and a clear understanding of what resonates with its audience. Their content leans heavily into athletics- aligned with their national reputation- and maintains a cohesive, recognizable brand.
Content Themes
Sports-heavy posts dominate the feed, reflecting UA’s identity and audience expectations
Strong school pride messaging, often tied to major wins, traditions, and high-profile athletic events
Consistent use of university branding, including colors, logo, and the iconic elephant mascot
Engagement Style
High engagement across posts, especially athletics-related content and student-feature videos
Videos that follow a “story-like” format help humanize the brand and make campus life feel active and dynamic
Student involvement is a key driver of engagement- UA frequently features students in fun, energetic videos filmed around campus
Strengths of Strategy
Content is visually cohesive and instantly recognizable
Relies on what UA is known for (athletics) to maximize reach
Uses emotionally resonant content- school pride, traditions, and community energy- to strengthen loyalty and drive share

Considerations &
Recommendations

Recommendations:
Instagram
1. Refine an Already Strong Visual Brand
Auburn’s Instagram already maintains a cohesive and visually appealing brand, but there is room for slight improvement
Establish even more consistent graphics, fonts, and templates across posts to enhance brand recognition
Important caveat: some inconsistencies come from collaboration posts with other Auburn departments, whose differing templates automatically appear on the main feed
Provide shared branding guidelines or co-branded templates to help partner departments align visually
Why it matters: Strengthening an already cohesive visual identity makes the feed more appealing to prospective students and supports long-term brand consistency
Expected outcome: A more polished, unified feed that drives higher engagement

2. Increase Engagement Through Instagram Tools
Expand use of interactive features such as polls, story questions, sliders, etc.
Polls, in particular, have proven to increase engagement (supported by U.S. Chamber of Commerce research)
Add more interactive story content to encourage participation from current and prospective students
Why it matters: Interactive elements make the account feel more student-centered and dynamic
Expected outcome: Engagement rates rising above the already strong 3%

Recommendations:
Facebook
1. Tailor Content to Facebook’s Older Audience
Auburn’s Facebook audience is largely composed of parents and middle-aged/older alumni, so content should reflect their interests—tradition, community pride, faculty accomplishments, and major athletics moments
Trend-focused or youth-oriented content from other platforms doesn’t translate as well here
Why it matters: Aligning content with this demographic strengthens engagement and improves overall sentiment

2. Regularly Audit What Resonates
Continuously review which posts perform well versus which receive negative reactions
Recent patterns show strong engagement for Iron Bowl posts and faculty awards, while topics like construction updates or AI in classrooms draw pushback
Why it matters: Understanding audience reactions allows Auburn to refine its Facebook content calendar more effectively
3. Emphasize Nostalgia and Human-Interest Stories
Develop more posts that invite alumni to reminisce, spotlight long-serving faculty, or highlight meaningful community stories
These themes drive comments, shares, and stronger emotional connection- key drivers of Facebook engagement
Why it matters: This type of content aligns naturally with the values and interests of Auburn’s older audience, leading to more consistent positive engagement

Recommendations:
X (Twitter)
1. Adapt Content to Twitter’s Unique Voice
Twitter responds best to concise, timely, and personality-driven content; Auburn should increase academic highlights, research updates, and student achievements while also adopting a more personable tone
Incorporating light, humorous replies to students or trending conversations- similar to the University of South Carolina’s approach- can humanize the account and make it feel more connected to campus culture
Auburn’s current presence is heavily athletics-focused, which leaves an opportunity to engage an unreached audience interested in academics, campus updates, and relatable interaction
Expected outcome: Broader audience reach, improved relatability, and a stronger academic & community identity on Twitter

2. Increase Original Academic & Campus Storytelling
Create more original tweets featuring academic achievements, faculty research, and student stories, paired with compelling visuals and curiosity-driven descriptions
Reduce reliance on retweets, so Auburn University’s own voice appears consistently throughout the feed
Why it matters: Original content performs better, strengthens brand voice, and allows Auburn to shape its own narrativeExpected outcome: Higher engagement, more shares, and increased visibility for Auburn’s academic strengths


Recommendations:
Linkedin
Strengthen Career-Focused and Alumni-Centered Content
Auburn already performs exceptionally well on LinkedIn, demonstrated by its high engagement rate (11.4%)  and strong audience response, making this platform one of its biggest strengths
Continue elevating that success by tailoring posts even more toward LinkedIn’s core purpose:  professional networking, career development, and alumni connection
Feature more alumni spotlights, “Where Are They Now?” posts, mentorship opportunities, and career-path storytelling to deepen engagement
Why it matters: LinkedIn’s audience is highly responsive to professional narratives, and expanding this content strengthens Auburn’s reputation as a career-driven, outcomes-focused university
Expected outcome: Increased comments and shares, stronger alumni–student networking, enhanced brand awareness, and greater word-of-mouth credibility in professional spaces

Recommendations:
TikTok
Increase Posting Frequency to Maximize Visibility
Auburn’s TikTok content is performing very well-  the platform has a staggering 11.3% engagement rate, the second-highest of all their channels
However, the account currently posts only about 3-5 times per month, which limits how often the algorithm can push content onto “For You” pages
Increasing frequency to 2-3 posts per week would give strong content more chances to be seen, significantly boosting impressions and total engagement
Competitors like Alabama have many more followers (172k), likely due to their higher posting frequency
Expected outcome: Higher reach and visibility without needing to change the content style that’s already resonating with viewers

Conclusion & References

Conclusion
Overall, Auburn University’s social media presence demonstrates strong reach, high visibility, and a solid foundation for engaging diverse audiences across platforms.
Each channel contributes meaningfully to the broader goals of awareness, desire, and loyalty, supported by steady follower growth and consistent engagement. While the university excels in storytelling, community pride, and academic representation, there are clear opportunities to strengthen platform-specific strategies, align content more intentionally with audience needs, and build greater coherence across its digital identity.
By refining the purpose of each platform, improving content cohesion, and leveraging the unique behaviors of each audience segment, Auburn can continue to reinforce the university’s mission, values, and long-term strategic vision across all social media channels.
